# Salvation Army papers and year books, 1965-1993

Mission reports of the time from inside the City.

- **Status:** catalogued on 2026-11-17 by the source search of 2026-11. Nobody has read these items for the repo yet.
  "What it gives" is the short note of the agent that found the item. It is not a checked fact.
  Before you use a fact in the model, read it on the source and write it in this file with its
  page and a short exact quote.
- **Dates:** 1965-1984.
- **Items:** 10, of which 10 are open now. Value of the set: high.
- **Copyright:** see the licence of each item. Copyrighted files go in `local/`, which git ignores.
- **Stored in git:** this file only.

## Why this is primary

Each item says its class and the reason. An item that is "retrospective" is first-hand
testimony given later: tag its facts "retrospective (year)".

### top-missions-6: Please may I go to School?

- **Creator:** The War Cry (Canada and Bermuda edition), The Salvation Army, Toronto; no byline
- **Date:** 1967-06-10
- **Kind:** press
- **Class:** primary (A report in the Salvation Army's own weekly at the time the school in the City opened.)
- **Value:** medium
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © The Salvation Army; archive.org collection 'war-cry' (uploaded by archive.org staff); no licence stated
- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/war-cry-1967-Jun-10
- **Other links:** https://archive.org/download/war-cry-1967-Jun-10/1967-Jun-10_djvu.txt
- **Proof that it exists:** 'OFFICIAL ORGAN OF THE SALVATION ARMY IN CANADA AND BERMUDA', No. 4307, Toronto, June 10, 1967
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** Sue Hong, aged ten, lived in one room in the City with her two younger brothers and her father, an addict; her mother was dead. She carried water from outside the Walled City to earn money by washing clothes, and her father gave her twenty cents a day for food. Her request led to a scheme for 240 children: the Army bought a building in the City, newer than those around it, and the school opened on 1 March 1967 with no government support.
- **Notes:** Found with the archive.org full-text API (service_backend=fts, user_query '"Walled City"', collection war-cry: 28 hits, 9 about Kowloon). The repo holds none of them; missions-11 holds only the issue of 9 Feb 1963. The same issue has 'Chinese band campaigns' (Kowloon Central Band), not about the City. War Cry 1967-Jun-10 also names the Oriental Missionary Society in another item.

### top-missions-8: The Hong Kong Salvation Army: what's it like?

- **Creator:** V.P.D. (a reporter of the South China Morning Post), in The War Cry (Canada and Bermuda edition)
- **Date:** 1967-10-21
- **Kind:** press
- **Class:** primary (A report of the time by a Hong Kong reporter on the Army's work, with figures for the City.)
- **Value:** medium
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © The Salvation Army; archive.org collection 'war-cry'; no licence stated
- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/war-cry-1967-Oct-21
- **Other links:** https://archive.org/download/war-cry-1967-Oct-21/1967-Oct-21_djvu.txt
- **Proof that it exists:** Headline 'The Hong Kong Salvation Army: what's it like?' (The War Cry, October 21, 1967, page three)
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** Names 'the Walled City Project'. The City had an estimated 35,000 people on about one-fifth of a square mile; the average family earned HK$200 a month. The Army educated 240 children there with no outside aid; a sponsor paid HK$20 a month. It also lists the Army's other Hong Kong work in 1967 (five clinics, eleven schools, a shelter for elderly women, a hostel for 116 young men).
- **Notes:** The initials V.P.D. are at the end of the article; the full name is not given. The text can be a reprint of an SCMP article: check the SCMP of 1967.

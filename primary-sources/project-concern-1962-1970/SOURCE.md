### missions-10: Doctor Afloat: Hong Kong

- **Creator:** A. E. Woolley (photos and text), The Rotarian (Rotary International); scan posted by Rotary in China with a glossary by Herbert K. Lau (劉敬恒), 2015
- **Date:** 1965-09
- **Kind:** press
- **Class:** primary (A magazine report and photos of 1965 on Project Concern, whose Hong Kong work began with a clinic in the City.)
- **Value:** medium
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © Rotary International (The Rotarian); the glossary © Rotary in China
- **Link:** https://rotaryinchina.org/wp-content/uploads/vfb/2023/03/Kowloon-West-Rotary-Club-Project-Concern-1963-Doctor-Afloat-Hong-Kong.pdf
- **Other links:** https://gwulo.com/node/59628
- **Proof that it exists:** PDF page 1 (scan): "DOCTOR AFLOAT: HONG KONG ... Photos and Text by A. E. Woolley", footer "16 THE ROTARIAN"; page 2 footer "SEPTEMBER, 1965".
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** Dr James W. Turpin of Project Concern 'soon set up a one-room clinic in the vice-, dirt-, and disease-infected Walled City of Kowloon' (p. 19). The Hong Kong staff of 1965: 14 doctors, five nurses, three technicians and 23 helpers; most frequent diseases: tuberculosis, parasitic infections, trachoma, bronchitis, iron-deficiency anemia and opium addiction.
- **Notes:** 6 pages: pp. 16-19 of The Rotarian of September 1965 (scan, read as images), then a glossary (pp. 5-6) of 2015: 'The first medical clinic was begun operation in the Walled City in 1962' and Project Concern Hong Kong = 香港醫藥援助會, Turpin = 丘平醫生. The file name says 1963, but the scan is of 1965. The Rotary Club of Kowloon West gave the second clinic boat (HK$60,000). The staff and disease figures are for all the Hong Kong work, not for the City clinic only. Gwulo node 59628 (Project Concern Floating Clinic) cites projectconcern.org.hk, which refused the connection on 2026-11-16.

### missions-11: Christianity in the News (item on Dr. James Turpin, Hong Kong), The War Cry, February 9, 1963

- **Creator:** The Salvation Army (USA), The War Cry
- **Date:** 1963-02-09
- **Kind:** press
- **Class:** primary (A news item of the time in the Salvation Army's own weekly about a clinic in the walled city of Kowloon.)
- **Value:** low
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © The Salvation Army (USA); the archive.org copy was uploaded by a user (jakej, 2012) and states no rights
- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/war-cry-1963-Feb-09
- **Other links:** https://archive.org/stream/war-cry-1963-Feb-09/1963-Feb-09#page/n15/mode/2up/, https://archive.org/stream/war-cry-1963-Feb-09/1963-Feb-09_djvu.txt
- **Proof that it exists:** archive.org item 'The War Cry', volume 'February 09, 1963', collection war-cry; OCR text read in full.
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** In 1962 Turpin, a doctor and Methodist minister, moved his family to Hong Kong and opened a clinic in Kowloon's walled city that gives medical care, food and clothing; he also built a clinic on a junk for families on sampans. The same issue has a Hong Kong school-meals item (kitchen in Hung Hom) and Maj. Eva Cosby, a Salvation Army nurse in Hong Kong.
- **Notes:** Wikipedia ('James Wesley Turpin') cites this item. It is the only War Cry item on the City that I found; the War Cry collection on archive.org has many issues of 1950-1990, but I did not search inside it because the web-search budget of the session was used up. Next step: a full-text search of that collection for 'Walled City' and 'Kowloon' (the Salvation Army kindergarten of the 1980s). The archive.org copy is a periodical scan, not a book, but its uploader is not known; check the rights before a copy goes into the repo.

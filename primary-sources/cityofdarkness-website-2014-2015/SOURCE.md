### top-blocked-c-3: CARE FOR THE ELDERLY

- **Creator:** City of Darkness website (Ian Lambot and Greg Girard); speaker the Reverend Isaac Liu
- **Date:** 2015-08-14 (post); interview c.1991-1992
- **Kind:** testimony
- **Class:** primary (A minister who ran a school, clinic, chapel and old people's centre in the City speaks during the clearance; the post reprints the interview.)
- **Value:** high
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © Ian Lambot and Greg Girard
- **Link:** https://arquivo.pt/wayback/20180717051006id_/http://cityofdarkness.co.uk/feed/
- **Other links:** https://arquivo.pt/noFrame/replay/20180717051006/http://cityofdarkness.co.uk/feed/, http://cityofdarkness.co.uk/care-for-the-elderly/
- **Proof that it exists:** RSS item 'CARE FOR THE ELDERLY', pubDate Fri, 14 Aug 2015 14:51:48 +0000: 'I was born in 1935 and came to Hong Kong in 1950 from my native village in China.'
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** The CNEC Living Word Gospel Centre (from 1960) in a four-storey building on Lung Shing Road: chapel on the ground floor, a small clinic and a drug rehabilitation centre on the mezzanine, the Tak Shing school on the three floors above (more than 420 pupils at its peak, kindergarten to Primary Six). The centre took over the yamen in 1971 and left it in 1991; an Old People's Centre from 1979 with 184 members, open in the afternoons Monday to Saturday, with Sunday worship, afternoon tea, seasonal trips of about 90 people, a Community Chest donation of $30,000 a year.
- **Notes:** Same feed copy as top-blocked-c-1. Category: 'The People'. The interview says that about a third of the elderly members had moved out so far and that new premises of about 1,600 sq ft were in Kwun Tong, thus it was made during the clearance (about 1991-92): an inference. Check against City of Darkness Revisited (held).

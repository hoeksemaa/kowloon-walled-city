### top-missions-15: An open letter to Lam Chiu Hing, age 12, in Hong Kong's Walled City, from her World Vision sponsor-father in America

- **Creator:** Joe Ryan (the sponsor-father), in World Vision magazine (World Vision International, Pasadena)
- **Date:** 1973-10
- **Kind:** testimony
- **Class:** primary (A first-hand account of a visit to a child's home and school in the City, printed months after the visit.)
- **Value:** medium
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © World Vision; uploaded to archive.org by a private user (collection worldvisionmagazine, magazine_rack); no rights statement
- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/WVUS_Magazine_OCT_1973
- **Other links:** https://archive.org/stream/WVUS_Magazine_OCT_1973/WVUS%20Magazine%20OCT%201973_djvu.txt
- **Proof that it exists:** Masthead 'WORLD VISION / OCT '73'; headline 'An open letter to Lam Chiu Hing, age 12, in Hong Kong's Walled City'
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** A visit in May 1973: Gladys Donnithorne showed the group the City, and Mary Chan interpreted. Chiu Hing's home was an 8-by-12-foot room with a 5-by-5-foot kitchen alcove, shared with her mother and brother; its 'window' was 'about fifteen inches wide with bars across it', 'indeed a treasure in the Walled City'. The writer says many people live 'underground'. Her rooftop school was up six flights of stairs; sponsorship cost $12 a month.
- **Notes:** Found with the archive.org full-text API (collection worldvisionmagazine, query '"Walled City"': 11 hits, 10 about Kowloon). The text places the rooftop school 'among the H-block houses' (resettlement blocks?): it is not clear if the school was inside the City. The uploader is a private user, not World Vision: check the rights before a copy is kept. Chinese characters for Lam Chiu Hing: not in the source.

### missions-3: 信安佈道所: 認識堂會

- **Creator:** Hong Kong & Macau Lutheran Church (港澳信義會)
- **Date:** undated (read 2026-11-16)
- **Kind:** document
- **Class:** retrospective (The congregation's own short account of its move out of the City at the clearance, written later.)
- **Value:** medium
- **Language:** zh
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © 港澳信義會
- **Link:** https://hkmlc.org/chapel_hsin_on_home
- **Other links:** https://hkmlc.org/introduction
- **Proof that it exists:** First sentence: "信安佈道所於九龍城寨拆卸後，搬到附近一幢大廈內的二樓裏聚會。"
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** After the demolition of the City, the Hsin On chapel met on the second floor of a building nearby, where the space was too small; in the 1990s it moved to the 港澳信義會小學 in Tseung Kwan O (將軍澳). It gives no address in the City, no founding date and no names.
- **Notes:** It confirms that the Hsin On work of GRS file HKRS1078-1-53 ('Hsin On Centre of the Hong Kong and Macau Lutheran Church', 1987-1992, a known lead) was a chapel that lived on after 1993. The page does not say in words that the chapel met inside the City; it dates its move to the demolition. The church's history page does not name the City. Ask the church office for the chapel's records, its address in the City and old photos. Do not mix it up with the other Lutheran church, 基督教香港信義會 (ELCHK): its 70th anniversary book names a 九龍城 congregation of 1954, but no City work.

### top-missions-19: Cover Photo: Dateline ('Unique among the world's marginal urban areas is Hong Kong's "Old Walled City."')

- **Creator:** World Vision Magazine
- **Date:** 1964-03
- **Kind:** photo
- **Class:** primary (A cover photo and caption of the time on World Vision's ministry in the City.)
- **Value:** low
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open
- **Licence:** © World Vision; uploaded to archive.org by a private user; no rights statement
- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/WVUS_Magazine_MARCH_1964
- **Other links:** https://archive.org/stream/WVUS_Magazine_MARCH_1964/WVUS%20Magazine%20MARCH%201964_djvu.txt
- **Proof that it exists:** Running header 'WORLD VISION MAGAZINE / MARCH 1964'; 'Unique among the world's marginal urban areas is Hong Kong's "Old Walled City."'
- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** World Vision ran an evangelistic, educational and medical ministry in the City 'through aid to the West China Evangelistic Band' (Donnithorne's mission) and with other groups; the cover boy was found in this ministry. It claims 200,000 people, scarcely any police, low wages, opium houses and gambling dens around.
- **Notes:** The 200,000 figure is an exaggeration of the time; keep it only as the caption's claim.

- **Creator:** Claude H. Thompson (professor of systematic theology, Candler School of Theology, Emory University), in World Vision Magazine
- **Date:** 1969-02
- **Kind:** testimony
- **Class:** primary (A visitor's first-hand impressions of a walk through the City, printed at the time.)
- **Value:** low
- **Language:** en
- **Access:** open

- **Link:** https://archive.org/details/WVUS_Magazine_FEB_1969

- **Kind:** press
- **Class:** primary (A news item of the time from the mission body that funded her work.)
- **Value:** low
- **Language:** en

- **What it gives (finder's note, not checked):** Gladys Donnithorne, a missionary in the Orient since 1919, was honoured at a banquet in Hong Kong on 1 December 1972.

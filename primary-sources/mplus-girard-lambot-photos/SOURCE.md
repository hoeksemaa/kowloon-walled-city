# M+ photographs by Greg Girard and Ian Lambot, 1987–1991

The eight Kowloon Walled City photos by Girard and Lambot that M+ shows
online. The two photographers worked in the city for about five years before
the clearance, for the book *City of Darkness* (1993).

- **Link:** https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/makers/greg-girard/ and
  https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/makers/ian-lambot/
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-29.
- **Copyright:** "© Greg Girard" or "© Ian Lambot". Credit line "M+, Hong Kong".
- **Stored in git:** no. The catalogue pages and images are in `local/`, which git ignores.
- **Image size:** about 1000 px on the long side. This is the largest size that
  M+ stores online (the untransformed Cloudinary URL). The prints are much
  larger (up to 149.5 × 120 cm).

## Why this is primary

The photographers made these photos in the city at the dates given. The prints
are from 2013–2015, but the negatives are from 1987–1991.

## Objects

| Object | Photographer | Shot | Title | Use for the model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013.143 | Lambot | 1989 | Aerial View from the Southwest | Whole city and its edges. Same view as the Commons 1989 aerial. |
| 2017.412 | Lambot | 1990 | South Elevation | The whole south face from the park. You can count the storeys of each building. |
| 2017.410 | Lambot | 1990 | Caged Balconies | Facade detail: metal cages on balconies. |
| 2017.411 | Lambot | 1991 | Shared staircase linking three buildings | Inside: how stairs join buildings. |
| 2018.4 | Girard | 1987 | Tung Tau Tsuen Road facade | North edge: shop signs and flats on the main road. |
| 2018.2 | Girard | 1989 | Children on Rooftop | The roofs. |
| 2018.1 | Girard | 1990 | Alley View #2 | Inside an alley. |
| 2018.3 | Girard | 1990 | Alley View #3 | Inside an alley. |

Files in `local/`: `mplus-<object number without the dot>.html` and
`mplus-<number>_<M+ image id>.jpg`.

## Facts in the M+ text

The catalogue text for 2013.143 and 2018.4 says that the city "housed over
thirty-five thousand people in one square city block", with "approximately
350 buildings", "towers ten to fourteen storeys high". This text is written
by M+ in the 2010s, so it is not primary. The photos are the primary part.

# M+ photographs by Greg Girard and Ian Lambot, 1987–1991

The eight Kowloon Walled City (九龍城寨) photos by Girard and Lambot that M+ shows
online. The two photographers worked in the city for about five years before
the clearance, for the book *City of Darkness* (1993).

- **Link:** https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/makers/greg-girard/ and
  https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/makers/ian-lambot/
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-29.
- **Copyright:** "© Greg Girard" or "© Ian Lambot". Credit line "M+, Hong Kong".
- **Stored in git:** no. The catalogue pages and images are in `local/`, which git ignores.
- **Image size:** about 1000 px on the long side. This is the largest size that
  M+ stores online (the untransformed Cloudinary URL). The prints are much
  larger (up to 149.5 × 120 cm).

## Why this is primary

The photographers made these photos in the city at the dates given. The prints
are from 2013–2015, but the negatives are from 1987–1991.

## Testimony of Chan Cheung Chuen (陳祥全) in audio guide 8038

The page of 2013.143 (`local/mplus-2013143.html`) also holds the transcripts of the M+ audio
guides 8038 and 9038. In 8038, Chan Cheung Chuen (陳祥全), a former resident, speaks between
the words of a presenter. His words are first-hand testimony, retrospective (after 2021).
[`../retrospective-testimony-web/SOURCE.md`](../retrospective-testimony-web/SOURCE.md) gives
their main points: the move after his marriage, the map, the walkways, the soy sauce and the
fires. The details below are not in that file. The quotes are from the English transcript
unless the row gives the Cantonese one.

| Detail | Quote (exact) |
|---|---|
| As a child, he lived in Tung Tau Estate (東頭邨), outside the City. | "When I was little I lived in Tung Tau Estate, which isn’t part of the walled city." Cantonese: "我細個嗰陣喺東頭邨住" |
| He first went into the City with classmates, to visit a friend. They played hide and seek in its dark corners and streets. | "I first went there with classmates to visit another friend who lived within the walled city"; "The dark corners and the intertwined streets were perfect for hide and seek." |
| When he married, he looked for a small home. That was why he moved into the City. | Cantonese: "要搵一個蝸居，就搬咗入九龍城寨" |
| The upper walkways let people go from inside one building into the next. | "They meant you could move easily from the inside of one building to another." Cantonese: "人從一幢樓嘅中間穿過，去到另一幢樓" |
| Many couples in the Kaifong (街坊福利會) met in the City. | "There were many couples in the Kaifong—or Neighbourhood Welfare Association—who met in the walled city." Cantonese: "街坊福利會中，好多街坊都係夫妻檔" |
| One shout for help brought all the neighbours. A thief who did not know the lanes ended in a dead end. | "a single shout for help would alert everyone nearby immediately"; "otherwise they’d end up stuck in a cul-de-sac!" |
| He sometimes felt safer in the City than outside. | "I sometimes thought it was safer in the walled city than outside." |

The words of the presenter are secondary, for example that Chan's work was mechanical drawing.
They are in the twin.

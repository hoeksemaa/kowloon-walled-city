## 1. "Denizens of noted hellhole face Walled City eviction"

By Robert Benjamin. *The Baltimore Sun*, 30 Jun 1992. Dateline Hong Kong.
The day before the police removed the last holdouts.

- Buildings: "350 seven- to 14-story buildings interlaced by a maze of dark,
  shoulder-wide paths — byways bounded by open sewers, lined with frayed nests
  of illegal electrical lines and perpetually damp from dripping water pipes."
- Holdouts: "about 100 holdouts from among the Walled City's more than 30,000
  residents". They camped "in a roadside shelter by one of the few entrances
  to the city that remain unlocked".
- Compensation: "more than $300 million". "Many have been moved to public housing."
- Wang Ching, 41, "proprietress of a plastic factory": "This is China, not
  Hong Kong. The British government has no power to move us."
- Lam Yin Ming leads the reporter "down a long dark corridor, around several
  corners, up a half-dozen flights of stairs", past "a few one-man workshops
  still operating". At his locked two-room flat: "This is my home … It was a
  good place to live."

## 2. "HONG KONG: Law in the Jungle"

TIME, 7 Sep 1959. No byline. This is about 30 years before the peak. Use it
for the early low-rise city, not for the 1987 form.

- Alleys: "some of the widest are only 6 ft. across".
- Trade: heroin "at 5¢ a pinch", inhaled "through rolled paper tubes or
  matchbox funnels". Restaurants "serve dog and cat meat".
- Police: after a constable was attacked and a man stabbed, police "arrested
  150 people" in one night and began nightly patrols.

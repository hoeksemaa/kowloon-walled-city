## Why this is primary

It is the verbatim record of the debate. Lord Kennet describes his own visit
to the city. The government minister (Lord Goronwy-Roberts) gives the
official position and the works in progress in 1974.

## Limits

1974 is about 15 years before the peak. The city was still growing, and
Kennet says buildings were "10, 11 and even 13 storeys". Population figures
are estimates. Use it for the physical form of the lanes and services, not for
1987 numbers.

## Key facts

Column numbers are the printed Hansard columns.

- **Lanes (c463):** "nowhere more than 3 feet wide. Mostly they are 2 feet
  wide, in some places they are only 18 inches wide". Entrances are alleys
  "2 feet or 3 feet wide". "No wheeled vehicle can get in there".
- **Ground (c463):** "The alleyways are unpaved and of earth. Down the middle
  of each runs an open drain with the sewage running down it, because the
  site is on a slope".
- **Ground floors (c463–464):** "many factories: small spaces, roughly lit":
  weaving with looms, light engineering, metal goods, carpentry, food.
- **Water (c464):** from municipal standpipes outside, "carried in buckets",
  or from "illegal wells dug directly under the houses".
- **Power (c464):** "stolen from the municipal mains supply outside the city
  by illegal connections", with "'swags' and 'festoons' of naked electric
  cables" over the alleys.
- **Population (c464):** official count 27,000, made by counting windows.
  Kennet says officials put the "true population" at 45,000.
- **Neighbours (c465):** people "walk across a plank at the tenth storey over
  the street into the window opposite. In some places they do not even need a
  plank".
- **Growth (c466):** in 1963 "the buildings were only one or two storeys high
  and the alleys were the normal width, 8 feet to 10 feet of mud".
- **Air funnel (c466):** builders kept out of the airport approach funnel.
- **Rents (c467):** "about one-third below what they are in the rest of Kowloon".
- **Government works (c472–473, Goronwy-Roberts):** "Pumphouses are being
  installed and the open ditches have, since last year at least, been cleared
  twice daily". The Urban Services Department has sweepers, refuse bins,
  standpipes for street cleaning, "daily chlorination of wells" and public
  latrines. In 1973 the government "started to clear the sensitive areas on
  the fringe".

# UPI wire reports of 1984 and 1987: the reporters' history of the City

This file holds the secondary parts of two United Press International (UPI) reports on Kowloon
Walled City (九龍城寨), filed from Hong Kong (香港). Its twin is
[`../../primary-sources/upi-reports-1984-1987/SOURCE.md`](../../primary-sources/upi-reports-1984-1987/SOURCE.md).
The twin keeps what each reporter saw or heard at the time. For 1984, that is the blocks, the
trades, the community society, the population and the belief about the status of the City. For
1987, it is the plan and the count, the alleys and the shops, and the words of residents and
officials. Each reporter also told the history of the City, from 1843 to the 1970s. The
reporters did not see these events, thus these background lines are here.

## Why this is secondary

Each report is primary for what the reporter saw or heard at the time. But the history lines
tell of events before the report:

- the founding in 1843, the wall, the mandarin and his soldiers;
- the lease of 1898, the wording of the treaty, and the two orders-in-council after the lease;
- the departure of the mandarin in 1899, and the demolition of the old buildings;
- the demolition of the walls under the Japanese occupation;
- the building of tenements after the war;
- the height law of 1975;
- the earlier clearance plans and the protests from Peking (北京).

The reporters were not witnesses of these events, and they name no source for them. The
reputation lines ("long have found refuge", "after decades of looking the other way") are
general claims about past decades, with no witness and no date.

## What it rests on

- The two reports cite no source for their history.
- The 1984 report quotes the words of the 1898 treaty. It does not name the treaty, and it
  does not say where it got the words.
- The 1987 report names two orders-in-council: one "later that year" (1898) and one "two
  months later". It gives no date and no number for either order.
- INFERRED: the reporters took the history from earlier press reports, from the files of their
  agency and from official briefings. The reports do not say this.

## Limits

- The height of the wall: the 1984 report gives "A 13-foot-high wall". The 1987 report gives
  "The 25-foot granite walls". The two reports disagree, and neither names a source. Use the
  maps and photos of the time in the repo (see the leads).
- The 1984 report gives no year for the demolition of the walls. The 1987 report gives 1943.
- The status of the City under the lease: the 1984 report says that "The 1898 lease put the
  complex within the colony's territory". The 1987 report says that an order-in-council gave
  the Chinese officials "sovereignty within the walls of a fort". The press reports of 1959 and
  1992 in [`../press-articles-1959-1992/`](../press-articles-1959-1992/SOURCE.md) also
  disagree on this point. The text of the 1898 Convention can settle it.
- The dates of the orders: the 1987 report says "later that year" (1898) and "two months
  later". The 1984 report gives 1899 for the British action. The reports do not give the date
  of an order. Check the dates in the orders themselves.
- "Hong Kong police entered the area only in unusual circumstances" (1987) disagrees with three
  primary records. The TIME report of 1959 in
  [`../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/`](../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/SOURCE.md)
  tells of nightly patrols from 1959. The primary part of the 1984 report tells of patrols "in
  the past few years". A policeman in *City of Darkness Revisited* (p251) patrolled the City
  from 1985. The primary records win.
- The height law of 1975: the 1984 report names neither the law nor the two buildings that
  lost their top floors. The caption of CoDR p336 (written later) names a 14-storey building
  on Tung Tau Tsuen Road (東頭村道), built in 1975 over the height limit. Do not take it as one
  of the two buildings without a record of the time.
- "some 500 soldiers" and "the material to extend the airport runway" have no source and no
  date.
- "the mandarin's house": INFERRED, this is the Yamen (衙門). It stood in the City until the
  clearance, and it is the only building left in the park today (see
  [`../wsj-kwc-2014/`](../wsj-kwc-2014/SOURCE.md)). The report does not use the word.
- The reputation lines and "after decades of looking the other way" are the reporter's reading
  of the past. Do not use them as facts for 1987.

## Leads to primary sources

The reports cite no record. They name one treaty and two orders, and the repo can check some
points.

- The Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (展拓香港界址專條), 9 Jun 1898. The
  1984 report quotes its clause on the Chinese officials "within the city of Kowloon". The
  repo does not hold the text. Get it, and check the quote.
- The two orders-in-council that the 1987 report names. Look in the UK National
  Archives, for example the Colonial Office series CO 129 (Hong Kong).
- The height law of 1975 and the two cut buildings:
  - The HKU (香港大學) clipping of 9 Jun 1975, "Walled City high risers told to trim tops"
    (record `gm80r044d`), in
    [`../../primary-sources/hku-newspaper-clippings/`](../../primary-sources/hku-newspaper-clippings/SOURCE.md).
    It needs an HKU login.
  - The GRS (政府檔案處) file HKRS396-1-4, "DEVELOPMENT OF BUILDINGS - KOWLOON WALLED CITY"
    (2.1.1973 – 6.1.1976), in
    [`../../primary-sources/grs-catalogue-kwc/`](../../primary-sources/grs-catalogue-kwc/SOURCE.md).
    It is not digitised.
  - The GIS (政府新聞處) photo of 1975 of the 14-storey building on Tung Tau Tsuen Road (CoDR
    p336), in
    [`../../primary-sources/city-of-darkness-revisited/`](../../primary-sources/city-of-darkness-revisited/SOURCE.md).
- The earlier clearance plans and the protests from Peking. The repo holds the *China Mail*
  front page of 13 Jan 1948 (CoDR p330). It also holds two pages of the *Hong Kong Standard*
  (英文虎報) of 11 and 19 Jan 1963 (CoDR pp130 and 334).
- The fort and its wall. The repo holds the survey map of 1845 with the stone fort (CoDR p64)
  and the draft map of 1923 (CoDR p71). It also holds old photos of 1898 to 1933, and one with
  no date (CoDR pp68–73 and 328–329). They can check the height and the line of the wall.
- The demolition of the walls under the Japanese occupation. The repo holds no record of the
  war years. Search the GRS catalogue and the UK National Archives for files on the walls and
  on the Kai Tak (啓德) runway. The reports give no year for the work on the runway.

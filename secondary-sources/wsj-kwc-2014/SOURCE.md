# *The Wall Street Journal*, "Kowloon Walled City" interactive (2014), the secondary part

This file holds the secondary part of the web project of *The Wall Street Journal* on
Kowloon Walled City (九龍城寨), Hong Kong (香港), of April 2014. That part is the text of the
WSJ staff. It has the narration of each chapter and of each person, the history timeline, the
captions, the video descriptions, and the charts that WSJ made of old figures. The words of
the residents and of the other witnesses are primary, tagged retrospective (2014). Their fact
boxes are primary too. They are in the twin file
[`../../primary-sources/wsj-kwc-2014/SOURCE.md`](../../primary-sources/wsj-kwc-2014/SOURCE.md).

## Why this is secondary

The WSJ staff wrote in 2014, more than 20 years after the clearance. They do not say that
they saw the City. The project has four kinds of part:

1. The words of a witness, in quotes, and the fact box of each person in "The People". These
   parts are primary. They stay in the twin file.
2. The narration: the "dek" of each chapter and of each person, the text of each map marker,
   the video descriptions, and the timeline. These parts are secondary. They are in this file.
   This is also true where the narration tells what a named witness did ("He completed his
   map in March 1987"). Those words are the reporter's, not the witness's.
3. The charts. A chart of a witness's own figures stays in the twin file. Examples are the
   prices of Paul Tang (鄧金祥), and the dim sum prices of Wong Chan Pui-wah (黃陳佩華),
   with the source "Wong Chan Pui-wah (1971)". WSJ did not show the records behind the other
   charts. Thus they are later charts of old figures, and they are in this file. They are the
   Kai Fong count of 1983, the population and area, the flat size of 1988, and the rehousing
   of 2002.
4. The photos. A photo by a witness is a record of its time (Suenn Ho, Rod Mason, Ko Tim
   Keung). Its caption is WSJ's text of 2014. The captions are in this file.

## Limits

- **The trade count.** The WSJ chart sums to 645. The study of 喬健等 (1992, pp1020–1034)
  gives the Kai Fong survey of 1983 as 745 premises (see `../../primary-sources/README.md`,
  "Secondary works"). The two totals differ by exactly 100. I did not see the record. Thus I
  do not know which total is right, or if the chart leaves out one trade.
- **The number of buildings.** The chart of "Explore" gives "500". The postman Lam Po-chun
  (林寶鎮) gives "350 (estimate)" (twin file). The primary figure wins.
- **The area.** "6.9 acres" is about 27,900 m². HK01 (香港01) of 2016 gives "2萬6千平方米"
  (see [`../hk01-kaifong-series-2016/SOURCE.md`](../hk01-kaifong-series-2016/SOURCE.md)).
- **The width of the streets.** The English says "none of them more than three feet wide".
  The Chinese of the same marker says "不超过一米宽" [not more than one metre wide]. Both are
  general claims. Use the plan of the repo for the width of each street.
- **The Tin Hau Temple (天后廟).** The English and the Chinese differ. The English: the garbage
  on the "tin grate above it" kept the sun off the temple. The Chinese: a wire mesh
  ("一道铁网") on the roof stopped the garbage that residents threw out.
- **The year 1992.** The English says "The last residents move out on July 2". The Chinese
  says that the clearance work started after the last residents left on 2 July. The English
  says "Many" residents went to Wong Tai Sin (黃大仙). The Chinese says "大多" [most]. *The
  Baltimore Sun* of 30 Jun 1992 found "about 100 holdouts" (see
  [`../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/`](../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/SOURCE.md)).
- **The demolition.** WSJ says that the demolition ended in April 1994, "just over a year after
  it began". HK01 of 2016 gives the start as 23 Mar 1993. The WSJ caption gives a "wrecking
  ball". The HK01 caption gives hammers ("鐵錘"). The two tools do not exclude each other.
- **The history of 1842.** The English says that the Qing "leases" Hong Kong Island "in
  perpetuity". The Chinese says "割让" [ceded]. Use a history work for the treaties.
- **The Kai Fong Association.** The Chinese text gives one more role: "为寨城内的房地产买卖作公证"
  [it witnessed the sales of property in the City]. The English gives only building works and
  posters.
- **The sanitation.** The Chinese text adds a sentence that the English does not have: no
  large epidemic in a community of 33,000 (see "Details").
- **The "How It Worked" text.** In the `life` file, the block `work.explainer` repeats the text
  of "City of Vice". The menu of the chapter gives the right text. This is an error of the data
  file.
- **The Gambling photo.** Its credit is "Joyu Wang/The Wall Street Journal". INFERRED: a photo
  of 2014, thus not of the City.
- **The pop chapter.** It tells of fiction and art. It says that *Bloodsport* (1988) "features a
  fight inside the Walled City". It does not say that the crew filmed in the City.
- **Dates.** Many markers give no date or only a decade ("starting in the 1970s"). Most of the
  vice markers tell of the 1950s–1970s, not of 1986.
- **The years inside.** The descriptions of the six resident videos give the years inside
  (see "Details"). For Wong Yum-fai they give 30 years, but his fact box gives 1972–1983 (the
  twin file records this). For Albert Ng they give 16 years. The credit of his quote in the
  "Today" part of the timeline gives "Years inside: 17".
- **The words of 1945.** The English gives them to "the colonial governor". The Chinese gives
  them to an official of the colony ("殖民地的官员").

**Schools, temples, the Yamen (衙門) (WSJ narration, secondary).**
- A church ran an "Aged People Centre" (老人中心) in the Yamen from the 1970s.

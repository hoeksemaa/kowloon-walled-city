| Historical, para 1 | Imperial officials used the site in the 15th century. It was first fortified in 1668 with a signal station. A small fort was built about 1810 at the head of the beach beside it. | "the site was used by imperial officials in the 15 th century and was first fortified in 1668 when a signal station was established there" |
| Historical, para 1 | The walled garrison city was built from 25 November 1846 to 31 May 1847. Stone walls with six watchtowers and four gates enclosed about 6.5 acres. | "Between 25th November 1846 and 31st May 1847, a walled garrison-city was constructed." |
| Historical, para 2; Chinese page | Inside stood the offices of the Commodore of the Dapeng Brigade (大鵬協副將) and of the Kowloon Assistant Military Inspectorate (九龍巡檢司), more than ten military buildings and some civilian houses. There were 250 troops at the start and more than 500 in 1898/99. | "250 at the time of the completion of the City, which increased subsequently to more than 500 in 1898/99" |
| Historical, para 3 | In 1899 British troops took over the City and expelled the Qing officials and soldiers. | "In 1899, however, British troops were sent to take over the City, and the Qing officials and soldiers were expelled." |
| Historical, para 4 | The wall was 15 feet wide, 13 feet high and 2,200 feet long. A second wall ran from the northern rampart to the top of the hill above the garrison. | "the wall's dimensions: 15 feet wide and 13 feet high for a gross length of 2,200 feet" |
| Historical, para 5 | After the war, high-rise tenements without approval and without proper foundations covered almost the whole site. The page names drug divans, hide-outs of criminals, vice dens and cheap unlicensed dentists. | "high-rise tenements built without authority and completely lacking proper foundations mushroomed cheek by jowl over almost the whole site" |
| Historical, para 6 | In 1987, with the agreement of the Chinese authorities, the government decided to clear the area and build a park. | "In 1987, with the agreement of the Chinese authorities, the decision was taken to clear the area and build a park on the site" |
| Historical, para 7; Chinese page | The demolition was complete in April 1994. The Chinese page says that the Antiquities and Monuments Office dug out the relics during the clearance. | "在清拆期間，一些寨城的遺跡被古物古蹟辦事處發掘出來" (during the clearance, some relics of the City were dug out by the Antiquities and Monuments Office) |
| Historical, paras 8-9 | The finds were the two plaques of the South Gate, the foundation of the wall, the foundations of the South and East Gates, and a flagstone path beside the drainage ditch at the foot of the inner wall. Three old cannons, stone lintels and couplets, and column bases were kept too. | "a flag-stone path next to the drainage ditch running along the foot of the inner wall" |
| The Old South Gate, Chinese page | INFERRED: The Chinese South Gate page says that in April 1994 the government spent a whole month knocking down the buildings with big hammers and bulldozers. The repo's other sources put the start of the demolition earlier, thus this month is only the end of the work (my inference). | "香港政府在一九九四年四月花了整整一個月，用大鐵鎚的推土方式，清拆寨城內缺乏監管和無穩妥地基的樓宇" (in April 1994 the Hong Kong Government spent a whole month, with big hammers and bulldozing, demolishing the unregulated buildings without sound foundations in the City) |
| The Old South Gate, Chinese page | The Chinese South Gate page says that visitors can see the reinforced concrete left from the buildings of the City, and that more than 30,000 low-income people lived there. | "亦可從當年遺下的樓宇鋼筋水泥，窺見在「三不管」年代，寨城內三萬多低下階層人士的居住環境" (one can also glimpse, from the reinforced concrete of the buildings left from those years, the living conditions of the more than 30,000 low-income people in the City in the 'three no-cares' era) |
| The Yamen, English and Chinese pages | The Yamen is the only building left of the City. It has three rows and four wings (三進四廂). Its walls and column bases are of brick and granite. Its roof is of China fir, with cylindrical and flat tiles. | "Its walls and column bases are built of brick and granite, while the roof is a traditional structure crafted of China fir" |
| The Yamen, Chinese page | After 1899 the Yamen had charitable uses: an Anglican poorhouse (安立間教會窮人院, 廣蔭院), an old people's home, a shelter for widows and orphans, a free school and a clinic. Last, before the clearance of the City, it was an old people's centre (老人中心). The English page does not give this. | "包括用作安立間教會窮人院（廣蔭院）、老人院、寡婦和孤兒收容所、義學、診所及在最後於寨城（除衙門）清拆前作為老人中心" (including use as the Anglican Church poorhouse (廣蔭院), an old people's home, a shelter for widows and orphans, a free school, a clinic, and last, before the clearance of the City (except the Yamen), an old people's centre) |
| The Yamen, Chinese page | The Chinese page says that ALMSHOUSE is the 廣蔭院, a place that took in and cared for poor old people with no family. | "【ALMSHOUSE】又稱【廣蔭院】，是收容及照顧貧苦無依老人的地方" ('ALMSHOUSE', also called 廣蔭院, is a place that takes in and cares for poor, helpless old people) |
| The Yamen, English page, para 3 | The English Yamen page says that the paths and pavilions of the park take the names of buildings and streets of the City. It names Longjin Road, Longcheng Road, Longjin Way, Xicheng Road, Guangming Lane and Guangming Road as street names of the 1950s. | "Longjin Road, Longcheng Road, Longjin Way, Xicheng Road, Guangming Lane and Guangming Road are, in fact, original street names from the 1950s." |
| The Yamen, English page, para 3 | The English Yamen page says that many drug addicts of the City kept their lamps lit day and night to smoke opium. It gives no source. | "many of the city's drug addicts kept their lamps lit day and night for taking opium" |
| The Yamen, English and Chinese pages; leaflet p. 2 | Inside the Yamen are stone tablets: the characters 墨緣 and 壽 by Chang Yu-tang (張玉堂), Commodore of the Dapeng Brigade (the name and title are from the 2018 leaflet), and the kan ke hui yi (刊刻會議) of 1886 (光緒十二年), a record of the duty roster and the pay of the Yamen troops. | "The kan ke hui yi, a record of the meetings for the duty roster and wages paid to the troops of Yamen, is also shown." |
| The Yamen, English page, last para | Two cannons made in 1802 (嘉慶七年) stand at the two ends of the front yard of the Yamen. | "Two cannons are positioned at either end of the Yamen's front yard. Made in 1802" |
| Design of the Park, English page | The park is in the Jiangnan garden style of the early Qing. Architects of the Architectural Services Department chose it after a visit to China. The design won a Diploma at the IGO Stuttgart EXPO 93. | "the project was awarded a Diploma at the IGO Stuttgart EXPO 93 (International Garden Exposition)" |
| Historical, paras 10-13; Chinese page | Building began in May 1994 with skilled artisans from the Mainland. It ended in August 1995. Governor Christopher Patten (彭定康) opened the park on 22 December 1995. It covers 31,000 square metres in eight zones and cost $76 million. | "the park was officially opened on December 22, 1995, by the Governor, the Rt. Hon. Christopher Patten" |

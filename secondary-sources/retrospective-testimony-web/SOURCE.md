visitors remember Kowloon Walled City (九龍城寨). Its twin is
[`../../primary-sources/retrospective-testimony-web/SOURCE.md`](../../primary-sources/retrospective-testimony-web/SOURCE.md).

a named witness did, saw or said.

This file keeps the other words of the reporters, editors and later writers. These are their
history of the City, their figures, their captions and one essay quote. It also holds one whole
article that is secondary: Hugh Farmer's article on the factories of the City, with the facts
W6 and W7.

## Why this is secondary

Each page gives the words of a witness, and the twin keeps them. But a reporter, an editor or
a host wrote the rest of each page years later. These writers were not witnesses. They add the
history of the City from 1668 to 1995, and round figures of the population, the area and the
compensation. They also add the dates of the clearance, and captions on later or borrowed
photos.

A line in which the writer tells what a named witness did, saw or said is not background. The
twin keeps it, as the twins of [`../wsj-kwc-2014/`](../wsj-kwc-2014/SOURCE.md) and
[`../hk01-kaifong-series-2016/`](../hk01-kaifong-series-2016/SOURCE.md) do. Some sentences mix
the two kinds. Then the twin keeps the part on the witness, and this file keeps the background.
For example, the twin keeps the work of Girard and Lambot from 1987 to 1992. This file keeps
the dates of the clearance from the same sentence.

Three parts need a note:

- Brian Coak was a witness in 1970 and 1974 only. His time line and most of his text tell the
  history of the City from 1668 to 1995. He did not see these events.
- bkshopr_IHB lived in the City as a child. But his lines on its size and its history ("It
  was built in the 20's") tell of things before his time or outside his view.
- Hugh Farmer's article retells the interviews of *City of Darkness* (1993). Farmer was not a
  witness. Only the comment of Sharon McAuley below it is first-hand, and the twin keeps it.

The narration of the M+ audio guide 8038 is also secondary. The file
[`../mplus-girard-lambot-photos/SOURCE.md`](../mplus-girard-lambot-photos/SOURCE.md) holds it,
with the words of the presenter around Chan Cheung Chuen (陳祥全). This file does not repeat it.

## Limits

- **The demolition.** The pages disagree. HK01 2024 (林羅平): "九龍城寨1994年正式清拆". HK01
  2016 (the tailor): 1993. 明周: "在一九九三年城寨完全清拆後". Shue Yan: "demolished in 1993".
  Beijing Visitor: "began in 1993 and was completed in 1994". The Travel Club: "began in March
  1993 and was completed in April 1994". Coak: "In 1993 the Walled City was beginning to be
  torn down". Posner (2016): "torn down in 1992". That is wrong: the demolition was 1993–94
  (moved from the twin on 2026-11-16). A primary record wins, for example the LegCo (立法局)
  Hansard in
  [`../../primary-sources/legco-hansard-1986-1993/`](../../primary-sources/legco-hansard-1986-1993/SOURCE.md).
- **The last residents.** HK01 (Girard and Lambot): "直至1992年最後一戶遷出". 明周: 1992, at
  the end of the years 1987–1992. Coak: "Evacuations started in 1991 and were completed in
  1992." Beijing Visitor: "The last residents left and demolition began in 1993". They
  disagree.
- **The population.** The figures are round, and no page names a count. 大紀元 gives "逾三萬",
  and Beijing Visitor gives "over 33,000". Coak gives 35,000 "By the early 1980s", and 50,000
  in 1987, on one page. The Travel Club gives 35,000 "By the late 1980s". bkshopr_IHB gives
  36,000.
- **The compensation.** Coak gives "HK$ 2.7 billion", and Beijing Visitor gives "HK$2.76
  billion". Beijing Visitor also gives "$380,000 being set aside for each of the individual
  flats". It names no source. In the twin, Lau Chun-fat got about HK$120,000 for his flat.
- **The murder.** 明周 gives a murder in the City in 1958. Coak gives 1959. TIME (7 Sep 1959),
  in [`../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/`](../../primary-sources/press-articles-1959-1992/SOURCE.md),
  is a record of that year.
- **The police raids.** Coak's text gives "over 3,000 police raids" in 1973–1974. His time
  line gives "Over 3,500 police raids" in 1973.
- **Errors in Coak's time line.** It gives "Wall is completed" for 1843 and again for 1847.
  It says that the convention of 1898 "leases Hong Kong to Britain", but his own text says that
  it gave the New Territories (新界). It puts the Japanese demolition of the wall in 1940, but
  the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong began in December 1941. He also writes "smitten to the
  ground in 1987".
- **Errors in Beijing Visitor and The Travel Club.** Beijing Visitor puts the Song outpost in
  "the 16th century". The Travel Club gives "the Sung Dynasty of 960-1297". The Song dynasty
  ended in 1279.
- **"In Cantonese, it was known as the City of Darkness."** (The Travel Club.) INFERRED: this
  is wrong. 黑暗之城 is the Chinese title of the book *City of Darkness*. It is not the usual
  name of the City.
- **Hugh Farmer's addresses.** "Shing Ngam Road" is not a street of the City. *City of
  Darkness Revisited* (p88) and the GRS (政府檔案處) catalogue put the golf-ball factory on Tai
  Chang Street (大井街) (fact A58). "Mung Chun Road" is not a street name of the City either.
  INFERRED: it is a garble of Lung Chun Road (龍津道). Farmer puts Lam Leung Po (林良寶) at "G/F
  58 Lung Chun Road", but fact B49 puts the factory off Tai Chang Street. He spells the names
  "Lam Leung Pom" and "Tai Kong", where the book has "Lam Leung Po" and the data has "Tai
  Kwong".
- **The data on W7.** Its `location_clue` glosses 城南道 as "Carpenter" Road. `data/context.json`
  gives 城南道 as South Wall Road and 賈炳達道 as Carpenter Road.
- **bkshopr_IHB's history.** He writes that "additional buildings were added on until the
  60s". 明周 says that many buildings went up in the 1980s. They disagree.
- **A mixed sentence in 明周.** The sentence on the 1980s (no piles, 14 storeys) goes on to the
  flat of Charles and Maggie (Z1, in the twin). It does not say whether Charles or the
  reporters give the 14 storeys. This file keeps the figures as background.
- **Captions.** Some captions of HK01 and 明周 stand on photos by Girard or Lambot, or on
  "網上圖片" (pictures from the web). The caption text is the reporter's, not the photographer's.

### usgov-16: Oral history interview with Dennis G. Harter (Foreign Affairs Oral History Project)

- **Status:** Read on 2026-11-20; a checker checked a sample of the facts on 2026-11-20.
- **Citation:** Dennis G. Harter, interviewed by Charles Stuart Kennedy, initial interview date 17 March 2004, Foreign Affairs Oral History Project, The Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training, copyright 2011, pp. 165-166 (table of contents p. 4); https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/Harter,%20Dennis%20G.toc.pdf (read 2026-11-20).
- **Class:** secondary (Second-hand: in 2004 (or a later session) Harter retells a friend's story of a visit to the City with the Hong Kong Political Adviser. The date of the visit is not given. It is not first-hand testimony, thus it belongs in secondary-sources/ (a twin folder oral-history-archives-1987-2024 there), not in primary-sources/.)
- **Date of the record:** 2004 (interview sessions from 2004-03-17); the visit that the friend told of is undated
- **Note:** The secondary part of the item of the same id in [`../../primary-sources/oral-history-archives-1987-2024/SOURCE.md`](../../primary-sources/oral-history-archives-1987-2024/SOURCE.md).

| Page | Fact | Words in the source |
|---|---|---|
| p. 165 | A friend told Harter that the Hong Kong Political Adviser had invited him to go with him to visit the Walled City. Harter heard the story the day before the interview session. The date of the visit is not given. | "he had been invited by the Hong Kong Political Advisor to go out and visit the so-called “Walled City.”" |
| p. 165 | Harter's background: the City was a small piece of territory that was 'somehow' left out of the maps of the lease of the 19th century, thus a lawless no-man's land. (His own version of the history.) | "somehow got omitted from the maps that were drawn up when the British leased these territories" |
| p. 165 | Harter's background: the City had no government and no police; only the local gangs that controlled the illicit activities had authority. | "It had no government, it had no police, it had no authority outside the local gangs who controlled illicit activities in the area." |
| p. 165 | Harter's background: it was a place of squalor and slums, where drug dealers and pimps operated freely. | "It was a place of squalor and slums where drug dealers and pimps operated freely." |
| p. 165 | The Hong Kong Government had rules on the height of buildings, so that they did not interfere with the flights into Kai Tak. | "the Hong Kong Government had regulations about how high buildings could be so they wouldn’t interfere with the flights" |
| p. 165 | An entrepreneur had built an apartment building in the City that rose a couple of storeys into the flight path. | "This entrepreneur had gone in and built something that would have been a couple of stories into the flight path." |
| p. 165 | The purpose of the visit was for the Political Adviser to go into the City and tell the builder to remove the top few floors of the building. | "tell the entrepreneur he had to remove the top few floors of this building" |
| p. 165 | The builder got a deadline. If he did not meet it, the Political Adviser promised that the whole building would be razed to the ground. | "he faced having the entire building razed to the ground" |
| p. 165 | Harter's view: although the British had no legitimate jurisdiction in the City, they felt they had the right to act there when the situation required it; the friend told the story to show it was a special case. | "the British actually had no legitimate jurisdiction, they felt they still had the right to operate there if the situation required it" |
| p. 165 | Occasionally the Hong Kong authorities crossed into the City in hot pursuit of a criminal. This was not common and needed a serious offence. | "Occasionally, in hot pursuit of a criminal, Hong Kong authorities would cross the boundary into the Walled City" |
| p. 166 | They also went in on a raid to find someone who was said to hide in the City's few blocks of tenements. | "as part of a raid to locate someone reputedly hiding in these few blocks of tenements" |

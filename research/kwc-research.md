# Kowloon Walled City, c. 1987-1990: research for the 3D model and simulation

Research date: 29 October 2026. Target state: the densest period, before the 1991-92 evictions.

## How to read this file

- Each fact has an inline source link and a confidence tag.
- **[H]** high: a primary source, a government source, or two good sources that agree.
- **[M]** medium: one good secondary source, or a search-result extract of a primary source.
- **[L]** low: a blog, an unsourced claim, or my own calculation from other data.
- "CoD" is *City of Darkness* (Girard and Lambot, 1993). "CoD Revisited" is the 2014 edition that you own.
- Many web sources repeat numbers from CoD. When a web page gives a CoD number, the book is the real source. You can check it in your scans.
- These sites blocked automated access: hkintexts.histsyn.com (declassified UK and HK government files), hub.hku.hk, journal.eahn.org, and parts of scmp.com and atlasobscura.com. I read Atlas Obscura, SCMP 2018 and the WSJ project through web.archive.org snapshots. For hkintexts, I used search-result extracts only, and I mark those facts "(extract)".

## 1. Geometry

### 1.1 Footprint size and shape

- **Shape: a near-parallelogram.** An 1898 survey gave it as 700 ft by 400 ft and 6.5 acres ([ZH Wikipedia 九龍寨城](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M]
- **Dimensions, other values:**
  - "about 210 by 120 metres" ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City), citing Sinn). [M]
  - 213 m by 126 m, on the plan in the SCMP graphic "City of anarchy", 16 March 2013, by Adolfo Arranz ([PDF](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - The 1843-47 wall was "700 feet long and 400 feet wide" ([Crawford, Atlas Obscura 2020](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
  - Cross-check: 700 ft by 400 ft gives a perimeter of 2,200 ft. The LCSD gives the wall a "gross length of 2,200 feet" ([LCSD](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/historical.html)). The two values agree. [M]
- **Area. The sources disagree by about 10%:**
  - 6.5 acres, about 26,300 m² ([LCSD](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/historical.html)). [H]
  - 2.6 ha ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - 0.026 km² in 1987 ([ZH Wikipedia infobox](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M]
  - 2.7 ha ([SCMP 2013 graphic](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - 2.8 ha (SCMP of 15 Jan 1987, quoted in [SCMP 2018](https://www.scmp.com/magazines/post-magazine/short-reads/article/2127786/when-death-knell-sounded-kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
  - 6.9 acres, about 27,900 m² ([WSJ interactive 2014, "Explore" data](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - "little more than 7 acres (2.8 ha)" (Graudin 2014, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - The pre-1943 Qing site was 70 mu, about 47,000 m². This includes land outside the walls ([ZH Wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [L]
- **Edges (from the exhibit plan in the park and the OSM roads):**
  - The NNW edge follows the curve of Tung Tau Tsuen Road.
  - The NE corner is rounded where Tung Tsing Road meets Tung Tau Tsuen Road.
  - The SSE and WSW edges follow the base of the old walls (Lung Chun Road and Sai Shing Road, see 1.7).
  - Sources: [Commons, exhibit plan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Streets_in_Kowloon_Walled_City.jpg) and [OSM](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/1650915). [M]
- **The 1990s fence line.** The demolition fence followed "almost exactly the line once marked out by the city's original granite wall" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
- **The official boundary** was drawn in red on PWD 1:600 survey plan 130-NW-1. It "tallies with the alignment of the City's original walls except for the Pak Hok Shan portion" ([Lai 2024, *Land* 13:2112](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]

### 1.2 Location, coordinates and orientation

- **Centroid, Wikipedia:** 22°19′56″N 114°11′25″E, which is 22.33222, 114.19028 ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
- **Centroid, my calculation:** 22.33207 N, 114.19025 E, which is 22°19′55.5″N 114°11′24.9″E. I used the OSM polygon of Kowloon Walled City Park ([OSM relation 1650915](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/1650915)). [L]
- **Park polygon, my calculation (OSM, ODbL).** The polygon is 33,400 m², against an official 31,000 m² ([LCSD](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/historical.html)). [L]
  - Bounding box: lat 22.33114-22.33322, lon 114.18894-114.19160.
  - Its smallest rotated rectangle is about 240 m by 164 m.
  - The park is larger than the old city, because it also covers former Sai Tau Tsuen land.
- **Orientation. The city is not aligned to north-south:**
  - Tung Tau Tsuen Road runs along the NNW long side. The segment beside the park has a bearing of 59.5° and a length of 222 m. [L]
  - Tung Tsing Road runs along the ENE short side, at a bearing of 148° for 170 m. [L]
  - Thus the long axis runs at about 60-65° (ENE-WSW). The "south" face looks SSE, toward Kowloon Bay and Kai Tak. [M]
  - The restored Yamen halls in OSM line up on an axis at about 165°/345°, so the Yamen faces SSE. This agrees with the south-facing feng shui layout in [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city). [L]
  - Modelling tip: on most plans, "up" is the Tung Tau Tsuen Road side, but that side really faces about 330°.
- **Site slope.** The site slopes. "Due to poor drainage on its sloping site, flooding was a frequent occurrence on the lower floors" ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). The north side is at the foot of Pak Hok Shan. Thus I expect the ground to rise toward Tung Tau Tsuen Road, but no source gives levels. [L]
  - I did not find spot heights (mPD) for the ground. See the open questions.

### 1.3 Boundary roads and neighbours

**Correction to the starting assumption.** Only two of the four roads in the brief touch the city.

- **NNW side: Tung Tau Tsuen Road (東頭村道).** It touches the city directly. Perimeter buildings had Tung Tau Tsuen Road addresses, for example "12/F, Room B, 31 Tung Tau Tsuen Rd." ([WSJ "People"](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [H]
  - The big public housing block across the road is Tung Tau Estate. See the CEDD aerial in 1.9 and [hkmemory 1982](https://www.hkmemory.hk/collections/hkplaces/All_Items/images/201107/t20110723_43453.html). [M]
- **ENE side: Tung Tsing Road (東正道).** It touches the city directly. There was a "Tung Tsing Road Sitting Out Area" at the east edge ([exhibit plan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Streets_in_Kowloon_Walled_City.jpg)). [M]
  - In 1963 the government tried to clear the "Sensitive Zone" on Tung Tsing Road, outside the east wall ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
- **SSE side: Carpenter Road (賈炳達道) is not adjacent.** It runs E-W about 60-90 m south of the park ([OSM](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/1650915), my calculation). [L]
  - In the late 1980s, a park lay between the city and Carpenter Road. This park was on the site of Sai Tau Tsuen, which was cleared in 1985 ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City); [Commons 1989 aerial](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kowloon_Walled_City_-_1989_Aerial.jpg)). [M]
  - Lai dates Carpenter Road Park to "c.1982" ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf), note 4). [M]
- **WSW side: Junction Road (聯合道) is not adjacent.** It runs N-S about 190 m west of the park's west tip (OSM, my calculation). [L]
  - Two-storey Sai Tau Tsuen bordered the city on the south and west until 1985 ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
- **The wider block.** The whole block bounded by Tung Tau Tsuen Road, Junction Road, Carpenter Road, Lok Sin Road and Tung Tsing Road became park. The exception is the SW corner, now a school, the Allegro housing and Kowloon City Plaza ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf), note 4). [H]

### 1.5 Heights, storeys and the airport height limit

- **History of the high-rises:**
  - Multi-storey building (4 storeys or more, reinforced concrete) "only began in 1963". By 1972, 71% of residents lived in blocks of more than 4 storeys ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf), citing a 1972 City District Office study). [H]
  - First 2-3 storeys, then 6-7 storeys, then "by the 1970s… buildings of up to 14 stories in height" (CoD Revisited extract, [Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
  - By the 1980s the buildings were 12-14 storeys ([Steadman](https://philipsteadman.com/blog/city-of-darkness)). [M]
- **Typical height:** 10-14 storeys, "100 to 140 feet" ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - Other values: "stacked up to 15 stories high" (SCMP of 15 Jan 1987, quoted in [SCMP 2018](https://www.scmp.com/magazines/post-magazine/short-reads/article/2127786/when-death-knell-sounded-kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
  - Not all perimeter blocks were tall. The ceremonial first blow of the demolition hit "an eight-story tower block on the edge" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
- **The low exception: the Yamen.** The Yamen area "remained an exception to the vertical development" and was "the sole substantial void… with visible sky above it" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
- **Height limit in storeys:** "13 to 14 stories" (Lambot 2007 p.48, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - "no more than 14 floors high to avoid collisions" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - "14 floors or 45 metres" ([Gigazine 2015](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20150627-life-inside-kowloon-walled-city)). [L]
- **Height limit in feet (UK file FCO 40/790, 1977; extracts only).** [M]
  - The ICAO height limits applied. The City lay across a **170 ft P.D.** zone and a **150 ft P.D.** zone.
  - About **31 buildings** were over the limit in 1975-77.
  - A building at **36-38 West City Road** reached **178 ft 6 in**, which was 28 ft 6 in over the limit of the 150 ft zone. Thus the west side was in the 150 ft zone. The files do not say where the zone line ran.
  - The number of over-height buildings was "frozen" from September 1976.
  - Sources: [p.18](https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/series/histsyn-fco40-engine/items/TNAG-0642-FCO40-790-Kowloon-Walled-City-and-aircraft-safety-in-Hong-Kong-1977?p=18) and [p.94](https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/series/histsyn-fco40-engine/items/TNAG-0642-FCO40-790-Kowloon-Walled-City-and-aircraft-safety-in-Hong-Kong-1977?p=94).
  - "P.D." is Principal Datum, the Hong Kong height datum. Thus the limit is a roof level above the datum, not a storey count. The real storey limit depends on the ground level of each plot.
- **Enforcement:** from 1975 a police detective sergeant kept a monthly report, "Construction in Walled City". It listed site numbers, streets, "original number of storeys" and "storey reconst[ruction]" ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
  - The government asked for the "rooftop water tank and top 1.5 floors of 19 Shung Yee Lane" to be removed (same source). [H]
- **Distance to the airport:**
  - Kai Tak was "800 metres" south (Lambot 2007 p.206, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - My calculation: the Kai Tak reference point (22°19′43″N 114°11′39″E) is 557 m from the centroid, at a bearing of 133.5°. The runway heading was 134° true ([Kai Tak Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tak_Airport)). Thus the city lay almost on the extended runway 13/31 line. [L]
  - Checkerboard Hill (22°20′06″N 114°11′04″E) is 681 m from the centroid, at a bearing of 298°. [L]

### 1.6 Floor heights and flat sizes

- **Floor-to-floor height:** no direct source.
  - The WSJ pair "10 to 14 stories" and "100 to 140 feet" implies about 10 ft (3.05 m) per storey ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [L]
  - Gerald Posner in 1987 saw a low alley ceiling with wires "less than a foot from the tallest cop's head" ([posner.com](https://posner.com/geraldposner/2016/05/tbt-reminder-of-my-1987-police-escort.html)). [M]
  - Measure the floor heights in the Japanese cross-section: one floor is about 1 inch tall at its scale ([archidose](https://archidose.blogspot.com/2019/01/grand-panorama-of-kowloon-walled-city.html)).
- **Flat size:**
  - Average 29 m², about 312 sq ft, from Hong Kong Lands Department 1988 data ([WSJ "People"](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [M-H]
  - "a typical unit was 23 m² (250 ft²)" (Basler 1992, Goddard, Lambot 2007, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - Resident examples, all from the same [WSJ "People"](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people) page. [H]
    - Room A, 7/F, 8 Sz Kung St: "more than 200 sq. ft.", bought for HK$50,000 in 1975.
    - 12/F, 31 Tung Tau Tsuen Rd: 200-300 sq ft, rented for HK$800-1,100 a month in 1985-91.
    - 8/F, 33 Lo Yan St: 500 sq ft.
    - Sai Shing Rd and Lung Chun Rd: 200, 600 and 500 sq ft.
    - G/F, Lung Chun Rd 2nd Lane: 400-500 sq ft.
  - Space per person: "40 sq ft" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M] CoD Revisited gives "7 square feet per person" for the 1970s ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). This low value is probably a ground-area ratio, so check it in the book. [L]
- **Upper floors:** the buildings had "wider upper floors, caged balconies and rooftop additions" ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). "Floors were cantilevered over alleys, sometimes until they touched those across the way" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
- **Density of floor space:** the floor-space index was "perhaps ten" times the land area ([Steadman](https://philipsteadman.com/blog/city-of-darkness), an estimate). [L]
- **Structure:** there were no "proper foundations or piling", and the "buildings held each other up" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). The foundations were often "thin layers of concrete poured into shallow trenches", and the buildings that leaned together were called "lovers' buildings" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [H/M]
  - The demolition used a wrecking ball, not explosives, because the blocks depended on each other for support (Hoyin Lee, [WSJ "Rise & Fall"](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=history)). [M]

### 1.7 Alleys and streets

**Widths and entrances**

- **Widths:**
  - "only 1–2 m (3–7 ft) wide" (Wesley-Smith 1998, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - "Mostly they are 2 feet wide, in some places… only 18 inches" (Lord Kennet, [Hansard, 11 Jun 1974](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1974/jun/11/walled-city-of-kowloon)). [H]
  - The main "streets" were "none of them more than three feet wide" ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - There were "gaps of only a metre or so between neighbouring buildings" ([Steadman](https://philipsteadman.com/blog/city-of-darkness)). [M]
- **Number of streets:** 20-30 streets and lanes ([ZH Wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M]
- **Number of entrances. The sources disagree:**
  - "66 entry points… all barely visible from the outside" (CEDD map, via [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). The WSJ data also gives entrance coordinates on its own non-geographic base map. [M]
  - In 1987, officials cordoned "the 83 streets and alleys leading into and out of the Walled City" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
- **Upper-level routes:** "one could travel north to south through the entire City without ever touching solid ground" ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). Chan Cheung Chuen mapped "the intertwined streets, and the upper-level walkways connecting different buildings" ([M+ audio guide](https://audioguide.mplus.org.hk/en/locations/kowloon-walled-cityaerial-view-from-the-southwest-2013143/)). [H]

### 1.8 The Yamen, open space, temples, wells, the wall and the gates

- **The Yamen (衙門):**
  - It is "in the centre of the Park". It is "the only building that remains of the Walled City" ([LCSD Yamen](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/his/yamen.html)). [H]
  - Layout: "three rows and four wings of houses", in brick and granite with a China-fir roof (same source). EN Wikipedia gives three halls: the office of the Assistant Magistrate in the middle hall, and his residence in the rear block ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City), citing LCSD). [M]
  - Two cannons of 1802 stand in front (same LCSD source). [H]
  - Size, my calculation from OSM: the halls cover an area of about 19 m by 48 m. The long axis is at about 345°. The roof area is about 550 m² ([OSM buildings](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/1650915)). [L]
  - On the exhibit plan, the whole compound, with its yard and the youth centre (青年中心), is larger. My rough estimate from the photo is 35-40 m by 55-60 m. [L]
- **Uses of the Yamen in the 1980s:**
  - An almshouse and old people's home. It was "leased to a missionary society in 1949" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H] ZH WP says a Christian group used it as a home from the 1920s. [M]
  - A church ran an "Aged People Centre" from the 1970s ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - The Hong Kong Federation of Youth Groups ran a youth centre (same source). [M]
  - The exhibit plan labels the Yamen 老人中心 (old people's centre) and the block north of it 青年中心 (youth centre).
- **Open space:** the Yamen yard was the one large courtyard. "Only the rooms on the perimeter had natural light" ([Steadman](https://philipsteadman.com/blog/city-of-darkness)). [M] The SCMP 2013 graphic also marks one "Courtyard" ([PDF](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
- **Lung Chun Free School (龍津義學):** the 1847 free school stood west of the Yamen. The later "Lung Chun Free School Building" was built above it ([exhibit plan caption](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Streets_in_Kowloon_Walled_City.jpg)). [M]
- **Temples:**
  - Tin Hau Temple: in the north part of the city, NE of the Yamen (exhibit plan). Garbage on the "tin grate above it" blocked the sun ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - Fuk Tak Temple: popular with the Chiu Chow residents (same WSJ source). [M]
  - A small temple near Lo Yan Street was "all but buried under the rubbish" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
- **Wells:** the park shows "three old wells" (Film Services Office, via [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). See section 4 for the counts of working wells. [M]
- **The wall (built 1846-47, removed 1943):**
  - Built "Between 25th November 1846 and 31st May 1847". It had "six watchtowers and four gates" ([LCSD](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/historical.html)). [H]
  - Size: 15 ft wide, 13 ft high and 2,200 ft in total length (same LCSD source). EN Wikipedia gives 4 m tall and 4.6 m thick. [H]
- **The gates:**
  - **South Gate:** the main gate "for more than a century". Its granite paving, foundations, a flagstone path and "a drainage ditch running along the foot of the inner city wall" were found in 1994. It stands beside the square in front of the Yamen ([LCSD South Gate](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/his/gate.html)). [H]
  - **East Gate:** linked to the shore by the Lung Tsun Stone Bridge (龍津石橋) ([ZH WP](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M] I found no details on the other two gates.

### 3.2 Where the businesses were

- **Dentists and doctors** faced outward, mainly onto Tung Tau Tsuen Road on the north edge.
  - "Several of the small shops on the right were operated by dentists" (photo from Tung Tau Tsuen Road, [Gwulo 22835](https://gwulo.com/media/22835)). [H]
  - Betty Ho, 1986: unlicensed dentists occupied the edge that faced "normal life" ([Chung Hwa](https://www.chunghwabook.com.hk/upload/files/2026/07_09/8961d31fc1bbfcc1dbd8f8cb3e13d50a.pdf) p.97). [H]
  - JA Wikipedia says this frontage was the most expensive land in the city ([JA WP](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍城砦)). [M]
  - "the streets were lined with illegal dentists, right in the open" (Sir David Akers-Jones, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Food premises** were mostly on Lung Chun Road and Tung Tau Tsuen Road in 1978 (Chung Hwa p.97). [H]
- **Factories on Kwong Ming Street:** factories moved into the former drug shops in the 1970s, and the street had several fish-ball workshops ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life); [ZH WP](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). Green dots on the exhibit plan mark the factory sites. [M]
- **Factory streets in CoD interviews:** Tai Chang St, Shing Ngam Rd, Lo Yan St, Lung Chun Rd, Lung Chun Back Rd, Mung Chun Rd and Sai Shing Rd ([industrialhistoryhk](https://industrialhistoryhk.org/kowloon-walled-city-2)). [H]
- **Street level:** "a mix of unlicensed dentists and doctors, market stalls and cafes that often included dog on the menu" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
- **Vice in the 1950s-60s:** gangs divided the city at Lo Yan Street (Jackie Pullinger, via Chung Hwa p.97). [H]
  - East of Lo Yan Street: heroin dens and brothels.
  - West of Lo Yan Street: opium dens, gambling stalls, small cinemas, brothels and dog-meat restaurants.

### 3.4 Triads, drugs and police

- **Gangs:** five triad gangs were active, "King Yee, Sun Yee On, 14K, Wo Shing Wo, and Tai Ho Choi" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
- **Lung Chun Road (龍津道)**, formed in 1951, was the centre for brothels, gambling and drugs. Its venues included mahjong, pai gow and fan-tan houses, dog-meat restaurants and opium dens ([ZH WP](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M]
- **Kwong Ming Street** sold heroin in the 1960s. "Brightness Street" got its name from the candle flames ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Strip shows and "little movies"** declined in the early 1960s (same WSJ source). [M]
- **1959-62 prices:** triad protection money was HK$5 a day; opium cost HK$5; heroin HK$0.30 (Paul Tang, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [M]
- **1973-74 raids. The sources disagree:**
  - "more than 3,500 police raids… over 2,500 arrests and over 1,800 kilograms… of drugs seized" ([EN WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - "逾3,000名警察" (more than 3,000 police) ([ZH WP](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/九龍寨城)). [M]
- **After 1974,** crime fell because the ICAC reduced police graft ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **1983:** the police declared crime "under control" ([EN WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
- **In the 1980s,** police patrolled "兩、三次" (2-3 times) a day ([mpweekly](https://www.mpweekly.com/culture/社會/黑暗之城-九龍城寨-城寨居民-239629)). [M]
- **1985:** sex "services" cost HK$15-30 (Rod Mason, via WSJ). [M]
- **Girard:** by the late 1980s the vice "wasn't any different really from… any working-class HK neighborhood" ([imprintlab](https://imprintlab.com/?p=10038)). [H]

### 3.5 Institutions

- **Kai Fong association:** the "Kowloon Walled City Kai Fong Welfare Promotion Association" was founded in 1963. It grew out of the 1962-63 anti-demolition committee ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life); [Chung Hwa](https://www.chunghwabook.com.hk/upload/files/2026/07_09/8961d31fc1bbfcc1dbd8f8cb3e13d50a.pdf) p.80). [H]
  - Its office was at 4/F, 46 Lo Yan St in 1974 (hkintexts extract). [M] The exhibit plan marks the association on the north edge (labelled 九龍寨城街坊福利事業促進委員會, near nos. 15-17).
  - It authorised building works, put up public-service posters, and witnessed property sales (WSJ; ZH WP). [M]
- **Schools:**
  - The Salvation Army ran a primary school and then a kindergarten ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - In 1975 there were 7 schools with about 2,100 children ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
- **Missions and clinics in 1975:** West China Evangelistic Board, Chang On Nursery (40 children), Project Concern clinic, a CNEC primary school (at the almshouses), a Salvation Army free clinic (at the almshouses) and a child-care centre (same source). [H]
- **Jackie Pullinger** arrived in 1966. She taught in a primary school in the City and opened a youth centre for addicts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Pullinger)). [H]
- **Post:** the government delivered the mail. Lam Po-chun worked inside from 1979 to 1991 on an 8-hour shift. He was one of only two postmen ([WSJ "People"](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [H]
  - Crawford writes that there was "only one postman" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]

## 4. Infrastructure

### 4.1 Water

- **Standpipes. The sources disagree:**
  - 8 standpipes: "one inside the city, and the others outside its perimeter" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). The WSJ says "only one official government standpipe… inside… and seven on its perimeter" ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). "Eight municipal pipes" also appears in EN WP. [H]
  - In 1977 there were 6 standpipes for 27,000 residents ([FCO 40/790 p.66](https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/series/histsyn-fco40-engine/items/TNAG-0642-FCO40-790-Kowloon-Walled-City-and-aircraft-safety-in-Hong-Kong-1977?p=66), extract). [M]
  - Use 8 for 1987.
- **Policy:** water came from "stand pipes placed outside the boundaries… and many illegal connections". In 1964 the government refused a mains supply to each flat ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
- **Wells. The sources disagree:**
  - "77 wells inside the city some 90 metres deep" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - "about 70 or so wells" (Gordon Jones, former district officer, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - 67 working wells with about 40 suppliers at the clearance, some over 100 m deep (CoD, via [blog](https://arch1932010-kwcandcrusoe.blogspot.com/2010/01/water-shortages-in-walled-city.html)). [M]
  - The government chlorinated the wells daily in 1975 ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
- **Distribution:** "Electric pumps delivered water to big tanks on rooftops. From there, water was funnelled through narrow pipes to the homes" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
- **Pump times:**
  - The pumps ran at fixed times, "usually noon and midnight" ([blog](https://survivalkitred32010.blogspot.com/2010/11/shelter-water-in-kowloon-walled-city.html)). [L]
  - Water came by floor in time slots ([HK01](https://www.hk01.com/社區專題/1033066/)). [M]
- **Fees:** factories paid HK$300 a month and homes HK$100 a month ([HK01](https://www.hk01.com/社區專題/1033066/)). [M] Each household paid a fixed monthly fee ([City in Time](https://www.cityintime.hk/en/article/carpenter-road-ca-1987/)). [M]
- **Leaks:** "Residents carried umbrellas to shield themselves from constantly dripping water pipes" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). The postman wore a hat ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]

### 4.2 Electricity

- **1974:** power was "stolen from the municipal mains supply outside the city by illegal connections", with "'swags' and 'festoons' of naked electric cables" ([Hansard 1974](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1974/jun/11/walled-city-of-kowloon)). [H]
- **1970 fire report:** "two heavy underground cables and an overhead entanglement of wires serve 90% of the premises" ([hkintexts](https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/page/1246846), extract). [M]
- **SCMP 2013:** "Electric wires were placed outdoors to prevent fires" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
- **Late 1980s:** households had their own supply and meters ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf); [City in Time](https://www.cityintime.hk/en/article/carpenter-road-ca-1987/)). [M] Illegal tapping went on: "We had to pay somebody to 'steal' electricity for us" ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Power company:** Kowloon was supplied by China Light & Power. Some web sources name Hongkong Electric, which is probably an error. Check the book.

### 4.3 Drainage, refuse and lighting

- **Drainage, 1974:** "Down the middle of each [alley] runs an open drain with the sewage running down it." The government said the ditches were cleared twice daily and that "Pumphouses are being installed" ([Hansard 1974](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1974/jun/11/walled-city-of-kowloon)). [H]
- **Drainage, 1980s:** "Drainage was insufficient and often blocked" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). The lower floors often flooded ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Refuse. The sources give two sides:**
  - From 1975, the Urban Services Department gave a daily collection of refuse and night soil ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
  - Bulky items were "hauled to the roof and abandoned" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - Refuse thrown from windows piled up on wire nets and grates over the alleys and temples ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city); WSJ). [M]
  - Many people kept cats to fight the rats ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Lighting:**
  - "A few of the streets were illuminated by fluorescent lights" (Graudin, via [EN WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [M]
  - By the 1970s there was "virtually no ground level daylight penetration save at its centre" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
  - Posner in 1987 saw "an occasional naked bulb" ([posner.com](https://posner.com/geraldposner/2016/05/tbt-reminder-of-my-1987-police-escort.html)). [M]
  - The Kai Fong association put in street lights after 1963 ([City in Time](https://www.cityintime.hk/en/article/carpenter-road-ca-1987/)). [M]

### 4.4 Stairs, lifts, interconnections and rooftops

- **Lifts:** there were only two lifts in the whole city, in about 350 buildings ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city); [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people); [Steadman](https://philipsteadman.com/blog/city-of-darkness)). [H]
- **Stairs:** buildings shared stairs, and "some residents had to cross their neighbours' homes to reach their own" ([City in Time](https://www.cityintime.hk/en/article/carpenter-road-ca-1987/)). [M]
  - "Corridors were connected to allow people to traverse different structures without returning to ground level" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
  - Photo reference: Lambot, "Shared staircase linking three buildings", 1991 ([M+ 2017.411](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/objects/kowloon-walled-cityshared-staircase-linking-three-buildings-2017411/)).
- **Rooftops:**
  - The roofs were "full of television antennae, clothes lines, water tanks and rubbish", and people crossed them on ladders ([EN WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Walled_City)). [H]
  - People used them "for exercise, playgrounds, relaxing and even pigeon racing" ([SCMP 2013](https://2014.sopawards.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/City-of-anarchy.pdf)). [M]
  - Children played and did homework on the roofs after school (Lambot, via EN WP). [M]
  - "The roofscape of the Walled City became its own public realm" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/how-hong-kongs-vertical-village-came-be/)). [H]
  - The postman used "Gangways and rusting metal ladders" to cross between roofs ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]

### 4.5 Aircraft

- **Approach to runway 13:** aircraft made "a low-altitude (sub-600 ft; 180 m) 47-degree right-hand turn" about "2 nautical miles (3.7 km) from touchdown". Pilots used Checkerboard Hill as the visual marker for the turn ([Kai Tak WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tak_Airport)). [M]
- **Position of the city:** Checkerboard Hill is about 0.7 km WNW of the city, and the airport reference point is about 0.56 km SE (my calculation, 1.5). Thus aircraft on the turn passed close to the city. [L]
  - I found no source with the real height of aircraft above the city.
- **Frequency:** "every ten minutes or so another jumbo jet descended" (Popham, via [M+ Magazine](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/exploring-kowloon-walled-city-photographic-journey)). [H]
- **Night curfew:** "A night curfew from 11:30 pm to 6:30 am" ([Kai Tak WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tak_Airport)). [M]
- **Visibility:** "passengers could see television sets" in homes during the turn (same WP source). [M]

### 4.6 Fire

- **1974-75 government view:** the maze stopped the fire services from bringing appliances "close to many of the buildings", and the new buildings "lack any form of fire protection" ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)). [H]
- **Record:** "there were no significant fires or outbreaks of disease" in 30-40 years (Gordon Jones, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]

## 5. Daily rhythms

No source gives a full hour-by-hour day. These are the time anchors I found.

- **Darkness all day:** "The area where they stayed was dark 24 hours a day" (Tam Sau-kuen, housewife, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - The sex workers "did not necessarily start work at night" (same quote). [M]
- **Water at night:** "There was no 24-hour water supply… even while we were sleeping at night, when we heard the sound of water dripping, we would immediately wake up and do the housework such as laundry and washing the dishes" (Tam Sau-kuen, same WSJ source). [M]
- **Factories:**
  - The golf-ball factory "operated twenty four hours a day" at its peak ([industrialhistoryhk](https://industrialhistoryhk.org/kowloon-walled-city-2)). [H]
  - In 1963, "The Walled City never sleeps" (Breslin, via [bluelotus](https://bluelotus-gallery.com/ian-lambot)). [M]
- **Child labour:** a fish-ball maker's son scraped fish after school until "差唔多9點幾10點" (about 9-10 p.m.) ([HK01](https://www.hk01.com/社區專題/1033066/)). [H]
  - Children wrapped sweets after school ([industrialhistoryhk](https://industrialhistoryhk.org/kowloon-walled-city-2)). [H]
  - Children helped in the family businesses ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [M]
- **Post:** the postman worked an 8-hour shift ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people)). [H]
- **Police:** "The police would patrol at certain hours, but they didn't stay… They just walked by" (Paul Tang, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
  - In the 1980s they patrolled 2-3 times a day, and a sign-in book hung at one shop ([mpweekly](https://www.mpweekly.com/culture/社會/黑暗之城-九龍城寨-城寨居民-239629)). [M]
- **Rooftops in the evening:** Girard described "the air and light and space on the rooftop in the cool of the evening… watching the big jets arc past" ([Feature Shoot](https://www.featureshoot.com/2020/02/welcome-to-the-kowloon-walled-city-one-of-the-most-misunderstood-places-in-history/)). [M]
- **Aircraft:**
  - A jet came about every 10 minutes ([M+](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/exploring-kowloon-walled-city-photographic-journey)).
  - There was a quiet period from 23:30 to 06:30 ([Kai Tak WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tak_Airport)). [M]
- **Sounds:** mahjong tiles ("The incessant click of mahjong tiles echoed along the walls", [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)), aircraft, hawkers, dripping water and machines ([City in Time](https://www.cityintime.hk/en/article/carpenter-road-ca-1987/)). [M]
- **Smells:** "Entire corridors were coated with the fine flour dust used for making noodles. Acrid, chemical smells filled the streets that lay alongside metal and plastic manufacturers" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/kowloon-walled-city)). [M]
- **Chess:** the WSJ video walk includes "Watch a game of chess" and "Play time" scenes (Suenn Ho footage, [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)). [M]
- **Water chores:** people queued at the standpipes on Tai Chang Street ([Wen Wei Po, 22 Oct 2023](https://dw-media.tkww.hk/epaper/wwp/20231022/a08-1022.pdf)). [M]
  - "每天媽媽外出打井水" (every day mother went out to draw well water) ([master-insight](https://www.master-insight.com/article/25635)). [L]
- **Meals:** one worker "rarely left KWC except to eat breakfast" ([industrialhistoryhk](https://industrialhistoryhk.org/kowloon-walled-city-2)). I found no other meal times. [H]

## 7. Existing 3D reconstructions and datasets

**Based on documents or surveys (useful for geometry)**

- **大図解九龍城 (1997):** a measured cross-section from the February 1993 survey. Its illustrator is 寺澤一美 and its supervisor is 可児弘明 ([Kinokuniya](https://www.kinokuniya.co.jp/f/dsg-01-9784000080705); [Impress 1997](https://internet.watch.impress.co.jp/www/article/970630/kowloon.htm); [archidose](https://archidose.blogspot.com/2019/01/grand-panorama-of-kowloon-walled-city.html)). This is the best vertical reference. The cut line is not documented online.
- **Bronze model in the park, near the South Gate:**
  - It shows the city as a whole. A separate bronze model shows one building facing the Yamen ([LCSD](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/parks/kwcp/fac/thousand.html)).
  - Photos: [Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Models_of_Kowloon_Walled_City).
  - Scale: one blog says 1:200 ([blog](https://leopard-robin-htsz.squarespace.com/blog/kowloon-walled-city-park)). [L]
- **Photogrammetry scan of the bronze model:** "Kowloon Walled City Sculpture Map" by MotPanda, made from 2,990 photos in Nov 2023, about 1 M triangles. It has no CC licence and carries a "NoAI" notice ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/kowloon-walled-city-sculpture-map-b2df5089c32440b7b03d8b056664c232)). Use it for reference only.
- **Official surveys:** PWD plans 130-NW-1 (1:600) and C-180-N7-1 (1974) ([Lai 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-13-02112/article_deploy/land-13-02112.pdf)).
  - Related work: Davies and Lai, ["Mapping a Chinese plan for the Kowloon walled city"](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/mapping-a-chinese-plan-for-the-kowloon-walled-city-an-initial-doc/), and [GeoJournal 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10708-025-11358-w).

The WSJ 2014 interactive uses this footage on a clickable route ([WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/20161228012123/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/)).
- **WSJ 2014 interactive:** it has a base map, 66 entrance markers, points of interest, resident profiles and the 1983 business census. The data is JSON at `getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=people|life|history` ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/http://projects.wsj.com/kwc/getData.php?f=grabChapter&chapter=life)).

## Open questions the book can answer

1. **The CoD building map.** How many buildings does it number? Can I trace each plot outline and its storey count from it? Does it match the park exhibit plan?
2. **Storey heights.** Does the book give floor-to-floor heights, or show sections with dimensions? Does the Japanese cross-section appear in *Revisited*, and where is its cut line?
3. **Storeys of each block.** Does the book give the storeys for each building? Which buildings were the two with lifts? Where did the 8-storey and lower blocks stand?
4. **The 1987 survey.** What are the full figures for residents, households, premises and businesses by type? These are in Goddard's chapter "The Clearance".
5. **The 1960s-70s vice survey** that Girard mentions. What are the exact counts of brothels, opium dens, strip clubs and dog-meat restaurants?
6. **Street routes.** Where did each street start and end? Which alleys passed under buildings as tunnels? Where were the 66 (or 83) entrances?
7. **Ground levels.** What were the ground levels (mPD) along the slope from Tung Tau Tsuen Road to Lung Chun Road?
8. **Upper-level routes.** Which upper floors and roofs held the north-south route? Where did the main bridges and ladders between buildings stand?
9. **Wells and standpipes.** Where were the wells, the 8 standpipes and the pump rooms? At what times did the pumps run?
10. **Where things were.** Where were the factories (fish balls, noodles, plastics, metal), the Salvation Army kindergarten, the other schools, the Tin Hau and Fuk Tak temples, and the Kai Fong office?
11. **Hours.** What were the work hours in the 32 resident interviews (fish-ball start time, shop hours, school sessions, meal times)?
12. **Electricity.** Which company supplied power, how did meters work in the late 1980s, and how many illegal connections were there?
13. **Butchers and illegal slaughter.** What does the book say about them, and where were they?
14. **Small structures.** Is the 1975 count of 563 buildings reconciled with "350 or so"? A 1974 briefing gave 199 buildings in a search extract, but I did not capture its URL.
15. **Gates.** What were the names and positions of the four 1847 gates, and the line of the wall under the 1980s buildings?
16. **Compensation.** Which total is correct (HK$2.7, 2.76 or 3.2 billion), and what were the rates per square foot?
17. **Aircraft.** How high did aircraft fly over the city? Do Girard's notes or captions give a height?
